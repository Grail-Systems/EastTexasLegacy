The East Texas water fight on one page

What’s happening, five facts with their sources, who’s who, and how to reach us. Every line here comes from a page on this site, and every page shows its sources.

The story

Data centers, crypto mines and power plants are lining up over the Carrizo-Wilcox Aquifer, the water Deep East Texas drinks. The East Texas Legacy Coalition is a grassroots coalition out of Angelina County. We ask the questions in writing, file the records requests, and put every answer where y’all can see it.

We’re not asking anybody to shut the lights off. We’re asking that drinking-quality water go to people, and that servers get cooled with lower-grade or reused water. The whole water fight, start to finish →

Five facts, with their sources

As of Nov. 22, 2026.

  1. One aquifer, a lot of straws.Your well, your neighbor’s well and the City of Lufkin’s wells are all straws in the same glass: the Carrizo-Wilcox Aquifer. Whatever a big straw takes, it takes from everybody.Source: City of Lufkin Water Production FAQ. See it on the water page →
  2. WattBridge asked for 1.5 billion gallons a year.It was approved for 80 million gallons a year in 2024–25 and never drilled. In March 2026 it asked the Pineywoods Groundwater Conservation District for 1.5 billion gallons a year. That request has been on hold since July.Source: Pineywoods general manager Wil Blough, email to ETLC, Sept. 22, 2026. See it on the water page →
  3. On Nov. 9, 2026, the district passed its new rules as written.The vote was unanimous; board president Kevin Gee wasn’t at the meeting. The rules exempt deep Wilcox wells, below 1,500 feet and pumping under 4,000 acre-feet a year, from pumping cutbacks. They also keep an exemption that lets a city resell water to a data center without the new data center monitoring.Sources: Wil Blough, written confirmation, Nov. 10, 2026; The Daily Sentinel. Read the full account →
  4. The official minutes said “concerns.” The recording says more.At the Lufkin Planning and Zoning Commission on Feb. 9, 2026, a speaker said, “You own the water rights, which means you own the choice.” The minutes call it “concerns.” We got the City’s own recording through a public records request.Source: City of Lufkin meeting recordings and minutes, released to ETLC under request CRR-2026-348. Listen to the recordings →
  5. It’s not just East Texas.Ground Truth Texas tracks 209 data center, crypto mining, power and industrial projects in 92 Texas counties. Of those, 16 data center, crypto and power projects have been stopped.Source: the Ground Truth Texas project list, with a source for each project. See the statewide map →
