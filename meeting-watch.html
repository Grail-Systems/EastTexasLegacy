    The rule, and the open question

    Since Sept. 1, 2025, Texas law says a meeting notice must be posted "at least three business days before the scheduled date of the meeting" (Gov't Code 551.043(a), as amended by HB 1522). The law doesn't say how to count those days.

    How we count: only full business days between the filed date and the meeting date. We skip weekends and the days the County is closed. That's the method the Texas Association of Counties recommends to its members. Every card shows exactly which days were counted and which were skipped, so you can count it your own way.

    Closed days: 2026 dates come from the Angelina County 2026 Holiday Schedule, approved by Commissioners Court Oct. 28, 2025. Dates before 2026 use the Texas state holiday list until we check the County's own 2025 list.

    On Nov. 24, 2026, we asked State Rep. Trent Ashby and State Sen. Robert Nichols to have a legislative committee ask the Texas Attorney General to settle how the days are counted. When there's an answer, it'll be here.

    How it works

    • Every hour it checks the County's agenda folder for new and changed agendas.
    • It saves a copy of each agenda with a digital fingerprint, so any later change to the file shows up.
    • It reads the Clerk's filed stamp and the printed meeting date. Before every run it checks itself against agendas with known answers, and if any check fails, nothing is published.
    • It never calls a notice "late" or "illegal." It shows the dates and the count, and you decide.

    Angelina County Commissioners Court is first. City councils, school boards and the groundwater district are next. The saved copies and the full data are public on GitHub.
