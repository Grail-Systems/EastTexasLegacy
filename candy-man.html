Sam Rayburn Reservoir · August 1973

The Candy Man and the lake

Every old-timer around Rayburn has heard some version of this one. Here’s what the record says, and why it isn’t finished.

1960s

The candy man of the Heights

Dean Corll grew up partly in Southeast Texas. He graduated from Vidor High School, near Beaumont, then settled in Houston. His mother ran a small candy business that became the Corll Candy Company, in a bungalow on West 22nd Street in the Houston Heights, directly across from Helms Elementary School. It closed in June 1968.

Neighborhood kids knew him as the friendly man from the candy factory who handed out free sweets. That’s where the name came from.

To the neighbors, he was a polite man who liked kids.

Sources: Dean Corll (Wikipedia, with its cited sources); “The Lost Boys,” Texas Monthly; “Houston’s Darkest Hours,” Houstonia; Houston Chronicle (a neighbor’s description).
1970–1973

Boys who didn’t come home

Over three years, teenage boys kept disappearing from the Heights and nearby. Many of the boys had gone to the same junior high.

Corll paid two teenagers from the neighborhood, David Owen Brooks and Elmer Wayne Henley, to bring him boys with offers of parties and rides. We won’t describe what happened to them after that. Their families have lived with it long enough.

Sources: Texas Monthly; National Center for Missing & Exploited Children, 2023.
Aug. 8, 1973

The morning it ended

Early that morning at Corll’s house at 2020 Lamar Drive in Pasadena, 17-year-old Wayne Henley shot him dead. Henley said Corll had threatened him and two other teenagers who had come over for a party. Then Henley called the police himself and started talking.

He and Brooks led officers to three burial grounds: a rented boat shed in southwest Houston, the beach at High Island on the Gulf, and the woods of our own corner of East Texas.

Sources: Wikipedia; CBS News; TIME, 1973.
Aug. 1973

What was buried at Rayburn

Corll’s family had a cabin by Lake Sam Rayburn. In the woods near it, in San Augustine County, Henley and Brooks showed deputies where four boys were buried. TIME reported the site was about 150 miles northeast of Houston. Brooks later admitted that one morning he and Henley went fishing at the lake before burying a boy there.

People came to Rayburn for the fishing. Some of them never knew what was out in the pines.

The four boys found near Rayburn were:

  • William “Billy” Ray Lawrence 15
  • Raymond Stanley Blackburn 20
  • Homer Louis Garcia 15
  • Michael Anthony Baulch 15

All four disappeared in the summer of 1973, the last summer of it.

Sources: KPRC, 2021 (San Augustine, four bodies); TIME, 1973; Texas Monthly (Brooks’s confession); Wikipedia (names, ages, burial sites).

Still open

The boy with no name

More than fifty years later, one of the boys has never been identified. He was found Aug. 9, 1973, was about 15 to 18 years old, and had likely been dead a year or more. In 2023 the National Center for Missing & Exploited Children released a new image of what he may have looked like, hoping someone would know him.

ML73-3356

If you know anything, or you think a missing uncle, cousin or classmate from the early ’70s could be him, contact the Harris County Institute of Forensic Sciences and give the case number ML73-3356, or call NCMEC at 1-800-THE-LOST.

After

What became of the rest

Henley was convicted of six of the murders and is serving life in a Texas prison; he was denied parole again in November 2025. Brooks was convicted of one murder, Billy Lawrence’s, sentenced to life, and died in custody in 2020. The Pasadena house was torn down in 2023. Searchers have gone back to the old sites more than once, looking for boys who were never found.

Sources: Wikipedia; ABC13, Texas EquuSearch search; Yahoo News, 2023 (house razed).
Why it’s here

Why we tell this one

These pines hold a lot of stories, and not all of them are pretty. This one passed from family to family around the lake for fifty years, and the details got fuzzy in the retelling. We checked it against the record so the facts and the names stay straight.
