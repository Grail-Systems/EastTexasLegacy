Hometown

Huntington

Before the town, there was Renfro Prairie and the farms around it. Then the railroad came through, and Huntington was laid out in a single summer.

1840

Renfro Prairie

Renfro Prairie, near present-day Huntington, takes its name from the Renfro family, who settled there around 1840.

Sources: Bob Bowman, ed., Land of the Little Angel: A History of Angelina County, Texas (Lufkin: Lufkin Printing Co., 1976).
1880s

The town that almost was

Before Huntington, there was Ora, between today’s town and the Angelina River. Ora had a school district by 1880 and, people said, the county’s first schoolhouse built of sawed lumber. Its postmaster named the post office in 1884 for his baby daughter, Ora Medford. When the railroad town came along, Ora’s families moved to it, and Ora’s school became the seed of Huntington’s.

In the late 1880s Lewis Baird and Alvin Russell tried to start a town about a mile east of where Huntington stands, near where the T&NO and Cotton Belt lines would later cross. It didn’t take.

Sources: Bob Bowman, ed., Land of the Little Angel: A History of Angelina County, Texas (Lufkin: Lufkin Printing Co., 1976), pp. 56–57, 163, 178, 293.
1900

Lots sold to the highest bidder

The Texas & New Orleans line came through, and promoters E. A. Blount and W. J. Townsend filed a plat in June 1900. On June 22 they auctioned the lots. Hundreds of buyers came, and the county history says there was plenty of liquor and a few fistfights by the tracks. The post office opened July 23, 1900, with Capt. R. D. Chapman as postmaster.

The town is named for C. P. Huntington, chairman of the Southern Pacific railroad.

Sources: Bob Bowman, ed., Land of the Little Angel: A History of Angelina County, Texas (Lufkin: Lufkin Printing Co., 1976).
1900–1902

Saloons, a lodge and two churches

Capt. R. D. Chapman, the first postmaster, ran a big general store, a piling and tie company and the undertaking business; the county history calls him the father of Huntington. Dr. C. B. Stewart was the first doctor. Before Prohibition the new town had seven saloons.

In 1901 Homer’s Masonic Lodge No. 254, the county’s first lodge, chartered in 1861, moved to Huntington. That same year First Baptist Church was organized under a brush arbor, and the Methodists started meeting in a store building in 1902.

Sources: Bob Bowman, ed., Land of the Little Angel: A History of Angelina County, Texas (Lufkin: Lufkin Printing Co., 1976), pp. 69, 75, 87–88, 164.
1900–1959

Schools on Renfro land

Huntington’s first school opened in 1900 on the site of the Renfro home, across the street from today’s school. From 1903 to 1913 it ran on John F. Renfro’s property. A two-story brick school followed in 1914, and the first 11th-grade class in 1921. From 1939 to 1959 the district was the Huntington Rural Schools, gathering in the country schools around it: Oak Flat, Odell, Rocky Hill, Salem, Ora and more.

John F. Renfro, the Renfro Prairie pioneer’s grandson, went on to the Texas House from 1927 to 1931. Read the Renfro story.

Sources: Bob Bowman, ed., Land of the Little Angel: A History of Angelina County, Texas (Lufkin: Lufkin Printing Co., 1976), pp. 35, 56–57, 299.
1907–1940s

A railroad town

In 1907 a short logging line, the Shreveport, Houston & Gulf, ran from the Carter-Kelley mill at Manning to Huntington. It quit in the 1930s after the mill burned. In 1917 the new state forestry department put one of its five district headquarters in Huntington.

Winnie Graham remembered trains stopping at Huntington until after World War II to load cotton bales and cattle and unload groceries, and the stationmaster’s mustard-yellow house across a lane from the depot.

Sources: Bob Bowman, ed., Land of the Little Angel: A History of Angelina County, Texas (Lufkin: Lufkin Printing Co., 1976), pp. 139, 173, 188–189; Winnie Graham, Under the Blackgum Tree: 165 Years in the Piney Woods (Stephen F. Austin State University Press, 2013), pp. 159–160.
1900–1940s

A railroad and cotton town

Huntington got telephones in 1907 and electricity in 1927, and incorporated in 1937 with Fowler Burris as its first mayor. By the 1940s it had about 1,000 people, three electric cotton gins and what one local called the largest cotton market in the county.

Sources: Bob Bowman, ed., Land of the Little Angel: A History of Angelina County, Texas (Lufkin: Lufkin Printing Co., 1976).
1953

A library for the men who didn’t come home

McMullen Memorial Library was dedicated June 21, 1953, in memory of 19 Huntington-area men who died in World War II.

Sources: Bob Bowman, ed., Land of the Little Angel: A History of Angelina County, Texas (Lufkin: Lufkin Printing Co., 1976).
1918–1974

A sports town

Huntington’s early boys’ teams were coached around 1918–22 by a woman, Charlie B. Adams. Annie Lee Jones coached the first girls’ team in 1929–30. The county history lists state titles in 1959, 1960, 1962 and 1974.

Sources: Bob Bowman, ed., Land of the Little Angel: A History of Angelina County, Texas (Lufkin: Lufkin Printing Co., 1976).
1911–1972

Huntington’s newspapers

Casper Ricks started the Huntington Herald in 1911. George J. Ivy’s Huntington Messenger came in 1920, the Huntington Weekly News in 1928, and in 1939 J. W. Cloud renamed it the Angelina County News. In 1972 Buddy and Jeanie Moore started the Huntington-Zavalla Herald.

Sources: Bob Bowman, ed., Land of the Little Angel: A History of Angelina County, Texas (Lufkin: Lufkin Printing Co., 1976), pp. 208–209.
1950

A newspaper fire

J. W. and Josie Cloud ran the Angelina County News in Huntington from 1939. In March 1950, a fire the county history calls mysterious destroyed their office and printing plant.

Sources: Bob Bowman, ed., Land of the Little Angel: A History of Angelina County, Texas (Lufkin: Lufkin Printing Co., 1976).
Our people

From the founder’s own family

Fawn Elizabeth Zion, who started the East Texas Legacy Coalition, has roots that run deep in this ground. Two of her people show up in the county’s own history book. Her family’s Oak Flat story, from Winnie Graham’s memoir, is on Under the black gum tree.

Great-great-great-grandfather
1942

W. D. “Bill” Russell

Born in Dallas County, Arkansas, in 1848, he came to Texas as a little boy of five and enlisted in the Confederate Army at Galveston. When he died in 1942, he was Angelina County’s last surviving Confederate veteran. He’s buried at Jonesville Cemetery near Huntington.

Grandfather
1957

Hayden Russell

W. D.’s great-grandson, born the year W. D. died. Hayden played on the 1957 Lufkin PONY League All-Stars, the team that won the PONY League World Series, beating Maywood, Illinois, 5–2 in the final.

Sources: Bob Bowman, ed., Land of the Little Angel: A History of Angelina County, Texas (Lufkin: Lufkin Printing Co., 1976); family records; PONY Baseball, list of World Series champions (1957: Lufkin, Texas, 5–2 over Maywood, Ill.).
