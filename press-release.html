Press release · For immediate release · Nov. 24, 2026

15 of 16 races had one name. Our families built this place, and we're taking it back.

East Texas Legacy Coalition offers free tools so any East Texan can protect the water, read the record and run for the seat that decides it.

Strictly nonpartisan: no candidates, no parties, no endorsements.

HUNTINGTON, Texas — On Nov. 3, 2026, 15 of the 16 county and precinct races on Angelina County's ballot had only one name on them. In May, 11 school and college board seats had one name, Lufkin's Ward 5 and Ward 6 races were canceled for lack of a challenger, and Diboll's whole city election was canceled. The people who decide our water, our land and our tax dollars are being chosen by default.

The East Texas Legacy Coalition (ETLC), a grassroots group of Deep East Texas families, wants to change that, starting at home. Its site, easttexaslegacy.org, now offers plain-language guides to every local seat, the public records behind the decisions being made about our water, and the history of the families who settled this land.

“Our families built this place, and we should have a say in what happens here,” said Fawn Elizabeth Zion, ETLC's founder and an eighth-generation Texan from Huntington. “We're their legacy. They meant for it to be that way. You don't have to be perfect. You don't have to know every little thing. It's OK to be new. But if we don't try, outsiders will come in and take it from us. In 1835, the people of Gonzales painted three words on a white cotton flag: Come and Take It. That's how we feel about our water.”

What we're working toward

  1. Protect the water. Keep the Carrizo-Wilcox Aquifer for drinking water and the shallow wells East Texas families depend on, and stop water from being taken just because it's there.
  2. Stand with landowners. Help landowners petition our local groundwater district to change the rule it passed Nov. 9, 2026, so families' wells come first. Nothing is ever filed in a landowner's name without their OK.
  3. Keep the public record public. ETLC files records requests, posts what it finds with sources, and keeps every record it publishes free.
  4. Put local government back in the people's hands. Through the civic leadership pipeline, every local seat has a page: what you'd actually do, the training you get, who can run and when to file. The goal is simple: no seat that touches our water, land or money goes unopposed.
  5. Follow the money. ETLC lays out who funds the officials making these decisions, using public filings and stating plainly that a donation is not proof of wrongdoing.
  6. Honor our heritage. Family stories, history, recipes and the wild foods of the Piney Woods, gathered from local families and sourced.
  7. Protect the whole living system. The pine forest, its plants and fungi, and the industrial projects that affect them, mapped and tracked as one ecosystem tied to the aquifer.
  8. Build a local economy that lasts. Keep the jobs we have and fix the rules, so growth pays its own way instead of borrowing from our wells. This county grew on cotton and timber. We want its future in local businesses that keep money here and make things that don't make us sick or hurt the land.
  9. Share the tools with all of Texas. Ground Truth Texas, ETLC's free statewide tracker built by Grail Systems, follows 209 large projects across 92 Texas counties, including 16 that have been stopped.

Run for it: the window is short

For the May 1, 2027 local elections, candidates file Jan. 13 – Feb. 12, 2027. That's 31 days. ETLC's new guide, “Every local seat in your county, and how to find them,” walks any Texan through finding every seat where they live in one evening, in any of Texas's 254 counties.

Who funds this

ETLC takes no developer money and posts who funds it. Its work so far has been paid for by its founder's own time and money and by small gifts from neighbors.

Three things you can do tonight

  1. Find your seat: easttexaslegacy.org/your-county-seats.html
  2. Join the coalition: our Facebook group
  3. Share this with one neighbor who'd be good at the job.

About the East Texas Legacy Coalition

The East Texas Legacy Coalition is a grassroots group of Deep East Texas families working to protect the Carrizo-Wilcox Aquifer, keep the public record open and celebrate the heritage of the Piney Woods. Its sites are built and researched by Grail Systems. ETLC is strictly nonpartisan on candidates: it endorses no one and backs no party.
