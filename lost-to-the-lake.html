Sam Rayburn Reservoir

Lost to the lake

Sam Rayburn Reservoir is beautiful. It also sits on top of a community, family farms and graves. Here’s what the county history remembers about what went under the water.

1860–1960

Concord: a hundred years, then gone

Concord grew up around a small plank church in 1860. About a hundred years later, the reservoir took it. The county history says every one of its more than 100 residents had to leave, and seven cemeteries were moved. Its school district was folded into Zavalla.

A log cabin that belonged to Col. T. L. Mott had to be bought back from the Corps of Engineers and moved about 12 miles, to Jasper County, to save it.

Sources: Bob Bowman, ed., Land of the Little Angel: A History of Angelina County, Texas (Lufkin: Lufkin Printing Co., 1976).
1950s–1960s

Farms and family land

The book’s family sketches keep mentioning the lake: land of the Moore and Cochran families, McGilbery Lake where Cornelius McGilbery settled, and the Sumrall family, whose graves were dug up from Parker Cemetery and reburied at Concord before the water came.

Sources: Bob Bowman, ed., Land of the Little Angel: A History of Angelina County, Texas (Lufkin: Lufkin Printing Co., 1976).
Still today

Graves that didn’t move

Moved cemeteries don’t always move every grave. The county history notes unmarked graves in more than one place, and one pioneer family cemetery that ended up inside a pipeline pump station near Redland. Federal relocation records could show which graves were moved and which were left behind. Those records can be requested.

Sources: Bob Bowman, ed., Land of the Little Angel: A History of Angelina County, Texas (Lufkin: Lufkin Printing Co., 1976).
Now

Why we tell this

Big water projects get sold as progress, and some of them are. But somebody always pays, and it’s usually the people who were already here. When the next project comes, the families in its path deserve to know first, and to have a say.

The lake has darker stories too. In 1973, police dug in the woods near Rayburn for the victims of Houston’s Candy Man. The Candy Man and the lake.

If your family was moved for Rayburn, we’d like to hear from you: contact@grailsystems.io. We’ll ask before we post anything.
