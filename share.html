For every East Texas family

Share your family’s story

The stories, the recipes, the old ways and the old photos. Let’s write them down while the people who remember are still here to tell them.

What we’re gathering

Anything your family has handed down. It doesn’t have to be polished, and it doesn’t have to be certain. Tell it the best you can, and tell us who told it to you.

  • Family storiesWho came here, how they lived, what they went through.
  • Legends and sayingsFolklore, ghost stories, weather signs, the things Granny always said.
  • RecipesEspecially the ones nobody ever wrote down.
  • The old waysFarming, canning, syrup-making, logging, hunting, quilting, home remedies (as history).
  • PhotosWith names, places and years if you know them.
  • RecordingsA grandparent telling a story in their own voice is worth more than anything we could write.

Our promise to your family

  • We ask before we post anything.
  • We credit your family by name, or keep it anonymous if you’d rather.
  • We never post anything about living people without their OK.
  • We don’t publish where family graves or home places are.
  • You can ask us to change or take down your story anytime.
  • Please send only photos and recordings your family made or owns.
  • Hard stories are welcome too. We check them against the record before anything goes up.

What to ask your elders

Sit down with a parent, grandparent or old neighbor. Ask first if you can record, then set your phone to record a voice memo. Start by saying the date and who’s talking. A few questions to get them going:

  1. Where were you born, and who were your people?
  2. What was the house you grew up in like?
  3. What did your family eat, and who did the cooking?
  4. What work did your parents and grandparents do?
  5. Where did you get your water when you were young?
  6. What did you do for fun?
  7. What church, school or store did everybody go to?
  8. What places are gone now that you wish people remembered?
  9. What stories did your grandparents tell you?
  10. What do you want your great-grandchildren to know?

What happens next

We read everything that comes in, write back to say we got it, and ask any questions before your story goes up. It may take us a little while, since every page is checked by hand.
