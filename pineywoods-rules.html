They Heard You. Then They Passed It Anyway.

What the Pineywoods Groundwater District passed on November 9, and why it matters.

By Fawn Elizabeth Zion, founder, East Texas Legacy Coalition

On Monday, November 9, the Pineywoods Groundwater Conservation District board unanimously passed its new rules exactly as written, after hours of public opposition. Those rules weaken pumping cutbacks for deep wells. They also keep an exemption that lets a city resell water to a data center without the new data center monitoring.

The East Texas Legacy Coalition asked the board, in writing and on the record, to close that exemption. They didn't.

What happened Monday

The board met at 1:30 p.m. at Kurth Lake Lodge. It held a public hearing on proposed rule changes and on five high-capacity well permits requested by Pitman Farms, followed by a business meeting.

Residents, landowners and water utility officials spoke for hours, most of them opposed. On November 3, the district's general manager wrote that his office was “inundated with unanticipated public comments.”

The board didn't act on the Pitman permits. Neighboring property owners have contested them, and a preliminary hearing will be held at a later date.

It did act on the rules. I called general manager Wil Blough at 8:56 a.m. on November 10. He confirmed the board voted on the rule amendments and passed them as proposed. I've sent him an email confirming that call, and I'm waiting on his written reply. The Daily Sentinel reports the vote was unanimous and came near the end of a meeting that ran about three hours.

The Daily Sentinel reports that about 150 people came to oppose the permits. Once they heard there'd be no action on the permits, most of them left before the board discussed and voted on the rules. The vote came about an hour after most of that standing-room-only crowd had gone. People who attended told us they left believing there'd be no vote that day.

The rule change

Rule 5.15 lets the district cut back or suspend big pumping when water levels drop. A 50-foot drop for three straight months can trigger it. A 75-foot drop brings more restrictions.

The new rule exempts deep Wilcox wells, below 1,500 feet, pumping under 4,000 acre-feet a year. Those wells still get monitored, but the cutbacks no longer apply.

Pitman Farms has asked for 3,363 acre-feet a year from wells 1,900 to 2,300 feet deep. That fits right under the line. The Daily Sentinel describes these as the rule changes requested by Pitman Farms.

The district's own hydrogeologist said the exemption is meant partly to steer industry toward deeper, more mineralized water instead of competing with public water systems.

The loophole that stayed open

The new rules require real-time monitoring wells for data centers. That's a real protection.

But the rule says it “does not apply to a retail public water utility if the water is used to provide for its service needs within its service area within the District.” Another rule, 7.1(b)(4), has a similar exemption.

In plain English: a city can pump groundwater and sell it to a data center inside its service area, and the data center monitoring never kicks in.

We asked the board to close that for new industrial sales over 100,000 gallons a day. They passed the rules without that change.

Why it matters in Lufkin

  1. 2009In a contract between the City of Lufkin and the mill's then-owner, Abitibi-Consolidated, a water addendum requires the city to supply up to 5 million gallons a day to the seller “or its assignee” for 20 years, with two 10-year renewals.
  2. Sept. 25, 2025In a letter to Amp Z, signed by City Manager Kevin Gee, the city calls the data center site “within our service area” and says it owns “several high-capacity water wells in proximity that could be utilized in serving this location.”
  3. March 6, 2026The 936 reports Amp Z's proposed 2.1-gigawatt AI data center, Project Lufkin, at the old Southland mill site.
  4. March 23, 2026District minutes record board president Kevin Gee saying the data center at the old paper mill would use City of Lufkin water.
  5. Nov. 2, 2026The city says it can't find Exhibit D-1, the district well permits that were supposed to be attached to the 2009 water deal. Whether that 5 million gallon reservation has been handed off to anyone is still an open records request.
  6. Nov. 2026The Lufkin City Council accepts Gee's resignation as city manager.

The district's own website lists Kevin Gee as board president, appointed by the City of Lufkin. A reporter who attended told us Gee was not at Monday's meeting. The minutes will confirm who was present and how each director voted.

Board treasurer Tommy Carswell is general manager of Four Way Special Utility District, a water utility in the district. The same exemption covers it.

Not the first time

District records we obtained through the Public Information Act show a buyer looking at land at 3641 Ben Dunn Road, on Highway 103 West, for a data center. He asked the general manager in writing how to avoid a public hearing. The general manager answered in writing, laying out the limits that trigger one.

The Pitman Farms application

Pitman Farms wants five wells for a poultry processing plant on FM 225, about nine miles west of Nacogdoches, near Lake Nacogdoches. That's 3,363 acre-feet a year, about 3 million gallons a day, or nearly 1.1 billion gallons a year.

The company's own hydrogeologist admitted its modeling predicts drops beyond the district's existing limits. He said those are pressure changes, not draining the aquifer, and that clay layers separate the deep Wilcox from the shallower Carrizo. He couldn't say whether the study used the updated groundwater model released in January 2026.

No one presented evidence at the meeting that these wells are meant for a data center.

What people said

Mary Reed, whose family has a well about two miles from the site, said the 4,000 acre-foot line “fits one application almost exactly.” She said the company's own report predicts pressure drops of 300 to 400 feet near the wells.

Charles Perkins, general manager of D&M Water Supply, said his utility strongly opposes the permits. He said 3 million gallons a day is nearly twice D&M's entire daily capacity and about 37.5% of what Nacogdoches pumps each day.

Others raised wastewater, truck traffic, odor, property values, and Lake Nacogdoches. Several said monitoring means little if the district gives up its power to order cutbacks.

Thank you to every neighbor, landowner and group who showed up, including Bobby Tillman, who just ran for state senate, and Jennifer Worley of Protect the Piney Woods.

What we did

The East Texas Legacy Coalition couldn't be there in person, so we did the homework. We sent written comments on October 31, before the deadline, and more on November 5 when new city records came in. The general manager confirmed in writing that both went into the public record and to the board.

To be fair

The agenda, posted October 16, said “action may be taken” and listed “Discuss and possibly approve rule amendments.” So they were allowed to vote Monday. The same agenda also listed possibly pursuing a change to the state law that created the district. We don't know yet whether they acted on that.

What we still don't know

How many directors were there and voted. How each one voted. Whether anyone recused. Whether they acted on changing the district's founding law. We've requested the minutes, the full recording, and the adopted rule text.

What you can do

  1. If you own land in Angelina or Nacogdoches County, you can petition the district to change its rules. Texas Water Code 36.1025 gives landowners that right. The district has 90 days to either start the rule change or put in writing why it won't. We'll help draft it. One petition is easy to ignore. Fifty aren't.
  2. If you live or own land near FM 225, call the district at 936-568-9292 and ask how to take part in the Pitman Farms permit hearing.
  3. Talk to the people who appoint this board: the Angelina and Nacogdoches County commissioners courts, and the Lufkin and Nacogdoches city councils. Kevin Gee's seat belongs to the Lufkin City Council. Show up at public comment and ask who they're putting on that board, and why.

The Pitman permits head to a hearing, with no date set yet. The regional groundwater planning group, GMA-11, meets November 18. The district board's next meeting is 1:30 p.m. Monday, February 8, at Kurth Lake Lodge, 1420 Kurth Lake Road, Lufkin.
