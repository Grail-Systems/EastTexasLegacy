Where Angelina County’s family records are

Most of what you need is closer than you think. Here’s the path we’d take, in order.

  1. Kurth Memorial Library, Lufkin: the Ora McMullen Genealogy Room. The county history calls it one of the most complete genealogy rooms in East Texas. Ask for three things: the countywide listing of every cemetery and its marked graves; the pioneer collection, which holds every family sketch submitted for the 1976 county history, including the ones that never made it into print; and the 1974–76 history committee’s research files.Source: Bob Bowman, ed., Land of the Little Angel: A History of Angelina County, Texas (Lufkin: Lufkin Printing Co., 1976), pp. vii, 86, 247, 317.
  2. The books. Land of the Little Angel (1976), especially Chapter 16, “Some Pioneer Sketches,” and its index. R. W. Haltom’s History and Description of Angelina County (1888; reprinted 1969), the first county history. Effie Boon’s 1937 University of Texas thesis, The History of Angelina County, which is free to read online and lists the old-timers she interviewed in 1934. Archie Birdsong Mathews’s 1952 UT thesis on the county’s economic development.Source: Bob Bowman, ed., Land of the Little Angel: A History of Angelina County, Texas (Lufkin: Lufkin Printing Co., 1976), pp. 62, 151, 205, 320–322.
  3. Old newspapers. Early issues of the Lufkin Daily News and The Lufkin News are searchable for free on the Portal to Texas History. In Huntington, the McMullen Memorial Library is worth asking about the old Angelina County News (1939–50) and Huntington-Zavalla Herald.
  4. Courthouse records. The Angelina County Clerk and District Clerk hold deeds, probate and court records back to 1846. The county history notes records from the Homer years survive at the Lufkin courthouse.Source: Bob Bowman, ed., Land of the Little Angel: A History of Angelina County, Texas (Lufkin: Lufkin Printing Co., 1976), pp. 213, 320.
  5. Confederate service and pensions. The Texas State Library and Archives keeps the index of Confederate pension applications, including widows’ pensions. The National Park Service’s Soldiers and Sailors database lists units. The county raised Company D of the 7th Texas Cavalry in 1861 and a second company at Homer in 1862.
  6. Land grants and surveys. The Texas General Land Office holds the original Spanish, Mexican and Republic land grants. The county’s first surveyor, William G. Lang, made its first land map in 1848.Source: Bob Bowman, ed., Land of the Little Angel: A History of Angelina County, Texas (Lufkin: Lufkin Printing Co., 1976), pp. 22, 281.
  7. Graves and markers. Find a Grave and the Texas Historical Commission’s Historic Sites Atlas cover many county cemeteries. Then go walk them, before the next road or pipeline does.

Walk the ground

Old cemeteries of the county

Most of the county’s first cemeteries began beside churches in the 1860s and 1870s. Others started when a family buried someone on their own land and later gave the ground. So many early graves are unmarked that nobody can say for sure which cemetery is oldest.

Walker CemeteryRedland, about 1855. Named for Thomas R. Walker.
Prairie GroveNear Diboll. Begun in 1849 with the graves of three Stovall children.
Bodan CemeteryPollok, 1850s. It straddles the old Smuggler’s Trail.
Homer Cemetery1860s, at the old county seat.
Largent CemeteryNear Bethlehem, 1862.
Page CemeteryNear Manning, 1863.
Jonesville CemeteryAbout three miles southeast of Huntington, at the second county seat. Home of the petrified-log marker.
Center CemeteryNear Huntington and the Rocky Springs community.
Old CenterAbout eight miles southeast of Lufkin, early 1880s.
The Havard cemeteriesFamily cemeteries of the Havard brothers; two near Saron date to the early 1870s.
Renfro CemeteryOn the old Renfro homeplace, Renfro Prairie (Beulah). John Folkner Renfro’s grave.
Gann CemeteryAt Clawson.

Not every grave stayed put. Before Sam Rayburn Reservoir filled, families’ graves were dug up and moved, seven cemeteries from Concord alone. And the county history records a pioneer family, the Brazils, buried in a cemetery that ended up inside the Gulf pipeline pump station at Redland. Unmarked family cemeteries are the most at risk when land changes hands. If you know of one, write it down and tell us. See Lost to the lake.

Sources: Bob Bowman, ed., Land of the Little Angel: A History of Angelina County, Texas (Lufkin: Lufkin Printing Co., 1976), pp. 85–86, 173, 255, 264, 273, 277, 299, 304–305, 309.

Our founder’s lines

The Renfros and Russells

Our founder, Fawn Elizabeth Zion, descends from families in both books: the Renfros of Renfro Prairie, who came in 1840 and started one of the county’s first schools, and the Russells, including W. D. “Bill” Russell, the county’s last Confederate veteran, and her grandfather Hayden Russell, who played on Lufkin’s 1957 PONY League world champions.
